- ## The four parts
	- **Relative's phone** - Linphone app, their own free linphone.org account.
	- **linphone.org** - free SIP service. Authenticates callers, wakes the app, relays the audio.
	- **Home server** - Asterisk in Docker, signed in to linphone.org as the *house* account.
	- **Child's phone** - corded analog phone on an adapter (ATA) that signs in to the home server.
- ## A relative calls the child
	- Relative dials the house username (example `samhome`).
	- linphone.org sends the call down the connection the server opened.
	- Asterisk reads the caller's username and domain.
	- On the allowed list -> the child's phone rings. Not on it -> hung up, the phone stays silent.
- ## The child calls a relative
	- Child presses one digit.
	- Asterisk looks up that button and calls the relative's Linphone account.
	- Any other number, or an empty button -> busy tone.
- ## Why nothing is opened on the router
	- The server makes one *outgoing* TCP connection to linphone.org and keeps it alive. Calls come back down it.
	- Audio is relayed by linphone.org, so both ends only send outwards.
- ## Decisions
	- Plain Asterisk, no FreePBX. Three short config files are enough for one phone.
	- Linphone-only for relatives: one app, no VPN, no phone number, no monthly cost.
	- A real phone number (SIP trunk) is possible later; the dialplan has a placeholder. See [[first-phone/Todo]].
	- Dropped: an account per relative on the home server. It forced every relative to install a VPN as well.
